# Lysosomal-enzyme-GAA

**https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/protein/NP_000143.2/**

Lysosomal alpha-glucosidase (GAA) is an enzyme that breaks down glycogen into glucose inside lysosomes, helping regulate glycogen storage and energy metabolism. It is essential for preventing excessive glycogen accumulation in cells, particularly in muscle tissues. Deficiency or dysfunction of this enzyme causes glycogen buildup and can lead to Pompe disease, a genetic disorder that primarily affects the muscles and heart.

# QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS

**1. What does your enzyme normally break down inside lysosomes?**
- Lysosomal alpha-glucosidase (GAA) breaks down glycogen, the stored form of glucose, into individual glucose molecules. It mainly cleaves α-1,4 glycosidic bonds and can also break α-1,6 linkages, allowing cells to recycle stored carbohydrates.

**2. What is the total length of your protein, and which amino acids are most common?**
- The human GAA preproprotein is 952 amino acids long. The most common amino acids in its reference sequence are leucine (L, 108) with 11.34%, proline (P, 81) with 8.51%, alanine (A, 73) with 7.67%, glycine (G, 73) with 7.67%, and lastly, valine (V, 67) with 7.04%.

**3. How many potential N-glycosylation motifs did you find? Where are they located?**
- Based on the figure, the GAA protein contains 7 potential N-glycosylation motifs distributed along its amino acid sequence. These motifs are located approximately at amino acid positions 140, 233, 390, 470, 652, 882, and 925. Each follows the N-X-S/T pattern, where X can be any amino acid except proline. These are only potential glycosylation sites; their presence does not confirm that glycosylation actually occurs.

NOTE: the numbers above are only estimates from figure 2

**4. Why can glycosylation matter for lysosomal proteins? Does finding a motif prove the protein is glycosylated?**
- Glycosylation is important because it can help proteins fold properly, remain stable, and reach their correct destination in the cell. For GAA, certain carbohydrate chains can receive mannose-6-phosphate tags that help direct the enzyme to lysosomes. However, finding an N-X-S/T motif does not confirm that glycosylation occurs. It only shows a possible site where a sugar chain may attach, and further testing is needed to verify it.

**5. What could happen in the cell if your enzyme did not work properly?**
- If GAA does not work properly, glycogen cannot be broken down efficiently and begins to accumulate inside lysosomes, especially in muscle cells. This buildup can damage cells and interfere with normal muscle function. A deficiency in GAA can cause Pompe disease, which may lead to muscle weakness and, in severe cases, heart and breathing problems.
